# Walk Game — Game Design

## 1. Design goals

The game must convert real-world movement into **visible, persistent, player-authored world change**.

The design is successful when movement creates anticipation before the player opens the app, and the app converts that movement into satisfying decisions rather than merely displaying fitness statistics.

### Primary fantasy

> I revived this world by moving through mine.

### Secondary fantasies

- I rebuilt places that once looked hopeless.
- I arranged the restored settlement my way.
- I can physically walk through the result of my long-term progress.
- My region keeps growing even while I am away.
- A large restoration goal can motivate another walk without punishing me if I rest.

## 2. Core resources

Keep the economy legible. Do not launch with ten currencies.

### Vitality

Primary movement-derived resource.

Sources:
- Verified steps.
- Capped activity-session bonuses.
- Milestone bonuses.

Uses:
- Start/complete restoration projects.
- Unlock districts/landmarks.
- Accelerate selected restoration work.

Vitality must never be purchasable for real money if the core marketing promise is that movement powers the world. Selling it directly would undermine the product fantasy and fairness.

### Materials

Produced primarily by restored infrastructure and idle systems.

Examples:
- Timber / Biomass.
- Stone / Salvage.
- Components.

Used for building restoration and upgrades.

### Knowledge

Produced by research/culture systems and story discoveries.

Used for Era 4–5 advancement, advanced upgrades, and new restoration methods.

### Cosmetic tokens (optional later)

If monetization is required, cosmetics are safer than selling movement progression. Keep cosmetic economy isolated from Vitality.

## 3. Progression layers

The player should always have at least one goal at each layer.

### Immediate goals — minutes
- Restore a bench, lamp, garden, bridge segment.
- Collect idle output.
- Move/decorate a building.

### Daily goals — hours/day
- Reach a restoration threshold.
- Complete one project chain step.
- Earn a movement badge.

### Weekly goals
- Restore a district.
- Finish a landmark.
- Unlock a new NPC or production chain.

### Long-term goals
- Complete a region.
- Reach a new world era.
- Unlock the next region.
- Transform a fully restored region into an advanced civilization.

## 4. Restoration progress

Avoid a single generic percentage driving everything. Use multiple visible systems.

Recommended region metrics:

- `ecologyScore`
- `infrastructureScore`
- `communityScore`
- `knowledgeScore`
- `landmarkProgress`

A region's overall Restoration Level may summarize these, but the player should see distinct causes and effects.

Example:

```text
Ecology       62%
Infrastructure 41%
Community      28%
Knowledge       8%
Landmark       35%
```

This creates meaningful branching: a player can revive nature before rebuilding industry, or vice versa, while the story can gate certain thresholds.

## 5. Restoration project types

### Micro restoration
Cost: small.
Duration: instant or very short.
Purpose: frequent feedback.

Examples:
- Clean fountain.
- Repair streetlights.
- Plant roadside grove.
- Clear rubble.

### Building restoration
Cost: medium.
Purpose: unlock production or services.

Examples:
- Greenhouse.
- Workshop.
- Water station.
- Research hall.

### Ecosystem restoration
Cost: medium/large.
Purpose: change terrain/ambience and unlock chains.

Examples:
- Refill river.
- Restore wetland.
- Reforest ridge.

### Landmark restoration
Cost: large multi-stage project.
Purpose: long-term motivation and story payoff.

Examples:
- Ancient tower.
- Dam.
- Observatory.
- Transit gate.

### Era project
Cost: very large and multi-resource.
Purpose: unlock a new technological/civilizational stage.

## 6. Restoration chains

Projects should form logical dependency graphs.

Example: River District

```text
Clear Intake
   ↓
Repair Pump Station
   ↓
Restore River Flow
   ├── Restore Wetland → Birds return → Nature Lodge
   └── Irrigate Fields → Farm unlocks → Market unlocks
```

The player should be able to preview at least one step ahead so walking toward a target has meaning.

## 7. Idle systems

Idle output is generated by functional restored structures.

### Rules

- Production continues while app is closed.
- Offline calculation uses last trusted timestamp and a capped duration.
- Initial cap target: 8 hours. Tune later.
- Production does not consume Vitality.
- Vitality remains the gating resource for major restoration.
- Idle systems must create choices, not replace walking.

### Example chain

```text
Restored Grove → Biomass
Biomass + Restored Quarry → Building Materials
Materials + Workshop → Components
Components + Research Hall → Advanced Restoration Parts
```

Keep chains short enough for mobile. Two to four processing layers is plenty.

## 8. Building progression

Each functional building can have 3 upgrade tiers initially.

### Tier 0 — Ruin
- Visible, unusable.

### Tier 1 — Restored
- Core function active.
- Building becomes movable.

### Tier 2 — Improved
- Better passive output or utility.
- Visual upgrade.

### Tier 3 — Flourishing
- Strongest base-game version.
- Cosmetic details/NPC activity.
- May contribute toward era threshold.

Do not require the player to rebuild the same building from scratch after moving it.

## 9. Builder customization

The builder is not a full zoning simulator.

Player actions:
- Select building.
- Move within valid placement area.
- Rotate in fixed increments.
- Upgrade.
- Place small decorations.
- Toggle paths/landscape elements where supported.

Constraints:
- Buildings cannot overlap.
- Critical paths and spawn zones cannot be blocked.
- Landmark footprints may be fixed or semi-fixed.
- NPC navigation must remain valid.

## 10. Third-person exploration

Explore mode exists to deliver emotional payoff, discovery, and world presence.

It is **not** a second full RPG.

MVP interactions:
- Walk/run/jump if desired.
- Talk to a few NPCs.
- Inspect restored landmarks.
- Find lore objects.
- Collect cosmetic discoveries.
- Trigger photo mode later.

Avoid combat until the restoration loop is proven. Combat can easily hijack the identity of the game.

## 11. NPC design

NPCs should make restoration feel inhabited without requiring a full life simulation.

MVP NPC states:
- Not present.
- Arrives when prerequisite restored.
- Roams along authored/navmesh areas.
- Has a short dialogue set that updates with region state.

NPC roles:
- Ecologist.
- Builder.
- Researcher.
- Merchant/crafter.
- Historian.

Later: small schedules and region events.

## 12. Region unlocks

Regions should unlock through **lifetime progression plus narrative prerequisites**, not only a single step number.

Example:

```text
Region 1: Ashfall Basin — starting region
Region 2: Withered Coast — 120,000 lifetime steps + restore Transit Gate
Region 3: Glass Plains — 300,000 lifetime steps + Era 2 reached
Region 4: Frozen Reach — 650,000 lifetime steps + restore Climate Relay
```

Exact values are placeholders and must be tuned against real player data.

Why use multiple prerequisites:
- Prevents high-step users from skipping the game immediately.
- Makes restoration matter as much as fitness volume.
- Gives story structure.

## 13. Movement milestone rewards

Milestones should be permanent and celebratory.

Examples:
- 1,000 lifetime steps: First Spark.
- 10,000: First Grove cosmetic.
- 50,000: Landmark restoration boost.
- 100,000: unique decoration.
- 500,000: region monument.
- 1,000,000: legacy structure.

Do not make every milestone a direct economy multiplier or balance will explode.

## 14. Daily design

Avoid coercive streaks.

Recommended daily system:
- Daily movement is always credited.
- 3 small optional "restoration prompts" rather than mandatory quests.
- Weekly consistency can reward 3, 4, or 5 active days.
- Missing a day does not reset lifetime progress.
- Rest days may still receive idle production and story/decoration opportunities.

## 15. Running/effort design

Running is an optional layer, not a requirement to compete with walkers.

Reward dimensions:
- Distance.
- Sustained movement duration.
- Cadence consistency.
- Personal improvement.

Avoid:
- Unlimited speed multipliers.
- Ranking players by fastest pace.
- Rewards requiring maximal effort.
- Encouraging sprints while holding/looking at the phone.

See `ACTIVITY_REWARD_SYSTEM.md` for formulas.

## 16. Failure and punishment

There should be almost no destructive failure in the core loop.

Never:
- Destroy buildings because the player did not walk.
- Remove resources because the player rested.
- Reset region progress.

Possible soft setbacks:
- A timed production boost expires.
- Optional challenge reward is missed.
- Idle cap is reached.

## 17. Monetization guardrails

If monetization is added:

Good candidates:
- Cosmetic building skins.
- Character cosmetics.
- Decorative packs.
- Photo-mode items.
- Optional story/cosmetic region expansions, if value is clear.

High-risk candidates:
- Buying Vitality directly.
- Paid movement multipliers.
- Loot boxes tied to movement performance.
- Ads targeted using activity/health data.

The product must remain credible as a movement game, not a pay-to-skip fitness app.

## 18. Telemetry for design validation

Collect only game telemetry necessary to balance the game, separate from sensitive raw activity data.

Useful aggregates:
- Vitality earned per day.
- Vitality spent per project category.
- Time between project completions.
- Building placement usage.
- Builder → Explore mode transition rate.
- Region completion time.
- Idle cap hit frequency.

Avoid retaining raw GPS traces unless the user explicitly starts a feature that needs them and the privacy design justifies storage. Prefer on-device computation and upload only derived values where possible.

## 19. Vertical slice content spec

Starting region: **Ashfall Basin** (working name).

Required content:
- 1 ruined central settlement.
- 1 dry river.
- 1 dead grove.
- 1 workshop ruin.
- 1 greenhouse ruin.
- 1 water station ruin.
- 3 houses.
- 1 research building.
- 1 transit landmark.
- 10–15 project nodes.
- 3 NPCs.
- 5 lore objects.
- 1 transition from gray/dead ambience to visibly living ambience.

The region must be satisfying enough that the team learns whether the entire concept works before building Region 2.

## 20. Design questions intentionally deferred

Do not block MVP on these:
- Final title.
- Final lore explanation for the catastrophe.
- Multiplayer/social systems.
- Combat.
- Seasonal events.
- Monetization implementation.
- Procedural generation.
- Full citizen simulation.

Validate the movement → restoration → builder → exploration loop first.
